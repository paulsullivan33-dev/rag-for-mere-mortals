Bramble Mk II Bench Polisher
Workshop Manual, third edition

About this machine

The Bramble Mk II is a bench polisher for
small metal and wooden parts. A 370 watt
motor drives two polishing wheels through
a single belt. The no-load spindle speed
is 2,950 rpm, the wheels are 150 mm in
diameter, and the machine weighs 11 kg.
It is built for a workshop bench, bolted
down through the four holes in its base.

Fitting the wheels

Unplug the machine before you change a
wheel. Slide the wheel onto the spindle,
fit the flange with its curved side
against the wheel, and tighten the nut
firmly by hand, then a quarter turn with
the spanner in the drawer under the
bench. The left-hand spindle has a
left-hand thread, so its nut tightens
anticlockwise.

Everyday use

Start the machine and let it reach full
speed before you offer work to the wheel.
Hold the piece with both hands and rest
your forearms on the bench edge. Use a
light touch: pressing hard slows the
wheel, overheats the piece, and wears the
felt pads out early. Work in short
passes, and turn the piece often so the
finish stays even.

Checking the belt

Check the belt tension every 40 hours of
running. Press the belt midway between
the pulleys with one finger. The belt
should move 8 to 10 mm. If it moves more
than 12 mm, slide the motor back on its
rails until the tension is right, then
tighten the two rail bolts. The drive
belt is part number BP-114.

Oiling and brushes

Oil the bearings every 100 hours of
running, using two drops of light
machine oil, ISO VG 32, in the oil cup
at each end of the spindle. Grease is
forbidden: it holds polishing dust and
wears the bearings faster than running
dry.

Inspect the carbon brushes at 500 hours.
Replace a brush when it is shorter than
6 mm. Brushes are supplied as a pair,
part number CB-500, and both must be
changed together.

Felt pads and compound

Replace the felt pads every six months,
sooner if the finish turns streaky. The
pads are part number FP-150. Use the grey
compound for cutting back and the white
compound for the final polish. Keep the
two compounds on separate pads: a pad
that has held grey compound will never
give a clean final finish.

When something is wrong

If the polisher vibrates in use, first
check that the wheel is balanced and
that the bench bolts are tight. A wheel
that has taken a knock can lose a chunk
of felt and shake the whole bench.

If the motor hums but the spindle does
not turn, the starter capacitor has
probably failed. The capacitor is part
number CP-77, and it lives under the
cover at the back of the motor.

If the finish is streaked even with a
fresh pad, the pad has picked up old
compound. Dress the pad with the rake,
or fit a new one.

Safety

Wear eye protection whenever the machine
is running. Tie back loose sleeves and
hair: a polishing wheel grabs cloth
faster than you can let go. Never leave
the machine running unattended, and
switch off at the wall when you finish.
The Bramble Mk II is not waterproof, so
keep drinks and coolant bottles off the
same bench.
